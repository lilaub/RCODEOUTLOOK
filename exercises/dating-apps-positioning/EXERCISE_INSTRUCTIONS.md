# Perceptual Mapping with Factor Analysis: Dating Apps Market

## Marketing Research Methods - Undergraduate Exercise

---

## Learning Objectives

By completing this exercise, students will be able to:

1. **Identify** relevant attributes consumers use to evaluate products in a category
2. **Collect and structure** perceptual data using rating scales
3. **Apply** factor analysis to reduce multiple attributes to key dimensions
4. **Interpret** factor loadings to understand what dimensions represent
5. **Create** data-driven perceptual maps from factor scores
6. **Analyze** competitive positioning and identify market opportunities

---

## Exercise Overview

**Duration**: 90-120 minutes (can be split into two sessions)
**Format**: Individual data collection, then class aggregation and analysis
**Tools Required**: Web browser, included Factor Analysis Positioning App

## Background: Why Factor Analysis?

In real-world marketing research, perceptual maps are rarely created by simply choosing two dimensions subjectively. Instead, researchers:

1. **Collect many attributes** that consumers use to evaluate products
2. **Have consumers rate** each brand on each attribute
3. **Use factor analysis** to discover the underlying dimensions that explain the pattern of ratings
4. **Create perceptual maps** using the factor scores

This exercise walks you through this entire process using the dating app market as a case study.

---

## The Dating App Market

The online dating market has exploded, with dozens of apps competing for users. Each app positions itself differently:

- Some focus on serious relationships, others on casual dating
- Some use algorithms, others emphasize user choice
- Some are free, others premium
- Some target specific demographics or interests

**Your Challenge**: Discover the key dimensions consumers use to perceive and evaluate dating apps, then map the competitive landscape.

## Part 2: Rating Matrix (25 minutes)

### Task 2.1: Rate Each App

For each dating app, rate it on each attribute using a 1-7 scale:
- **1** = Very low / Does not have this quality
- **4** = Neutral / Average
- **7** = Very high / Strongly has this quality

**Tips:**
- Rate based on your perception (or general reputation if you haven't used the app)
- It's okay to skip apps you truly know nothing about
- Try to use the full range of the scale
- Be consistent in how you interpret each attribute

### Task 2.2: Check Your Ratings

Before proceeding, review your ratings:
- Do apps that are similar have similar rating patterns?
- Do apps that feel different have different patterns?
- Have you used the full 1-7 scale?

## Part 3: Factor Analysis (15 minutes)

### Understanding the Analysis

The app will perform **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**, a form of factor analysis that:

1. Finds patterns in how attributes correlate with each other
2. Extracts "factors" (underlying dimensions) that explain these patterns
3. Each factor is a weighted combination of the original attributes

### Task 3.1: Run the Analysis

Click "Run Factor Analysis" and observe:

- **Variance Explained**: How much of the total variation do the first two factors capture? (>60% is good)
- **Factor Loadings**: Which attributes load heavily on each factor?

### Task 3.2: Interpret the Factors

This is the most important analytical step. Look at the factor loadings and answer:

**For Factor 1 (X-axis):**
- Which attributes have high positive loadings (>0.5)?
- Which attributes have high negative loadings (<-0.5)?
- What underlying concept might this factor represent?

**For Factor 2 (Y-axis):**
- Which attributes have high positive loadings?
- Which attributes have high negative loadings?
- What underlying concept might this factor represent?

**Name your factors** - give each a descriptive label that captures its meaning.

*Example interpretations:*
- Factor might represent "Casual → Serious Intent"
- Factor might represent "Mainstream → Exclusive"
- Factor might represent "Algorithm-Driven → User-Controlled"

## Part 4: Perceptual Map Analysis (20 minutes)

### Task 4.1: Examine the Map

Study the generated perceptual map:

1. **Clusters**: Which apps are positioned close together? Why might that be?
2. **Outliers**: Which apps occupy unique positions? What makes them different?
3. **Gaps**: Are there empty areas on the map? What would an app in that space look like?

### Task 4.2: Strategic Analysis

Answer the following questions:

1. **Market Leader Position**: Where are the most popular apps (Tinder, Bumble) positioned? What does this tell you about mainstream preferences?

2. **Differentiation Strategies**: Pick two apps that are positioned far apart. How do their positioning strategies differ?

3. **Competitive Clusters**: Identify a cluster of 3+ apps. How might an app in this cluster differentiate itself?

4. **White Space Opportunity**: Describe a hypothetical new dating app that would occupy an underserved position on the map. What would be its key features?

## Part 5: Reflection & Presentation (15 minutes)

### Task 5.1: Methodology Reflection

Answer these questions about the research process:

1. How did factor analysis help simplify the complexity of multiple attributes?

2. What are the limitations of perceptual mapping based on your own perceptions vs. surveying many consumers?

3. If you were conducting this research professionally, what would you do differently?

### Task 5.2: Managerial Implications

If you were a product manager for a new dating app:

1. Which position on the map would you target and why?

## Technical Notes for Students

### About Factor Analysis

**Factor analysis** discovers hidden patterns in data by finding combinations of variables that tend to move together. In marketing:

- If users rate apps high on both "exclusive" and "expensive," these might reflect a single underlying "premium" dimension
- Factor analysis finds these combinations mathematically

**Principal Component Analysis (PCA)** is a specific technique that:
1. Creates new variables (principal components/factors) that are uncorrelated
2. Orders them by how much variance they explain
3. The first two components often capture enough variance to create a useful 2D map

### Interpreting Factor Loadings

- **Loading > 0.5**: Attribute is strongly associated with high scores on this factor
- **Loading < -0.5**: Attribute is strongly associated with low scores on this factor
- **Loading near 0**: Attribute is not related to this factor

### Why Two Dimensions?

We use two dimensions because:
1. Humans can easily visualize 2D maps
2. The first two factors usually capture the most important variation
3. It forces strategic simplification (you can't position on everything)

## Instructor Notes

### Facilitation Tips

1. **Part 1**: Encourage diverse attribute generation; compile a master list if doing class aggregation
2. **Part 3**: Walk through factor interpretation together; this is where students often struggle
3. **Discussion**: Compare individual maps—differences highlight subjectivity in perception

### Common Student Questions

**Q: What if my factor analysis shows low variance explained?**
A: This might mean (a) you need more apps/attributes, (b) your ratings lack variation, or (c) the market truly doesn't have clear underlying dimensions.

**Q: My factors don't make intuitive sense.**
A: Try rotating the factors (the app offers varimax rotation) or reconsider which attributes might be noisy.

### Data Quality Tips

- Encourage students to rate only apps they have some knowledge of
- Suggest they research unfamiliar apps briefly before rating
- More attributes generally produce more stable factor solutions
